ADAR enzymes convert adenosine to inosine (A-to-I) in double-stranded RNA, and the cell reads
inosine as guanosine. One edit can change a protein without touching the genome: the Q/R site of
the glutamate receptor GluR-B is edited in nearly every transcript, and mice that cannot edit it
die of seizures. I wrote about why this matters for neurons in
[RNA and neural computation](/writing/2025-05-12-rna-and-neural-computation/). That essay ended on
a need: better methods to survey the whole transcriptome for functionally active A-to-I sites.

ModFormer is my answer to that need. Earlier predictors were each trained on one dataset, one
species, or one question: is this site edited, or how much? ModFormer is one model for all of
them. It reads sequence and RNA structure together, and returns two numbers for every adenosine:
how much the site resembles observed editing sites, and the predicted edited fraction.
The full study is out as a preprint on bioRxiv:
[ModFormer: A unified structure-aware model of transcriptome-wide A-to-I RNA editing](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.23.768611).

## The model reads a transcript as sequence and as structure

The animation above follows one region of human APP pre-mRNA through the model.

1. **Embed.** The RNA language model [RiNALMo](https://github.com/lbcb-sci/RiNALMo) turns the sequence into a learned representation.
2. **Fold.** [ViennaRNA](https://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/RNA/) predicts how the transcript pairs with itself.
3. **Pair.** A 64-nucleotide window around each candidate adenosine enters with the stretch it pairs to, as a single track and a pair track.
4. **Attend.** 24 [Pairformer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w) blocks, the architecture AlphaFold 3 uses for pairs of residues, mix the two tracks.
5. **Predict.** Two heads score every adenosine: an editing-site score from 0 to 1, and a predicted editing rate from 0 to 1.

Editing depends on the duplex an adenosine sits in, not only on its neighbors. That is why the
pair track exists: the model sees the base opposite each adenosine and the shape of the duplex.

## It beats published predictors on sites they were built for

I compared ModFormer with five published editing-site predictors and a simple neighbor-base rule
on four benchmarks. The score is the area under the ROC curve (AUC): 0.5 is chance, 1 is a perfect
ranking of edited over unedited sites.

| Benchmark | Sites | ModFormer | Best other method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human Alu duplexes | 729 | 0.93 | 0.94 (AdarEdit) |
| InoDREAM | 17,956 | 0.94 | 0.96 (InoDREAM, its own benchmark) |
| Invertebrates | 1,678 | 0.83 | 0.80 (AdarEdit) |
| Mouse Neuro-2a and brain | 21,667 | 0.97 | 0.66 (InoDREAM) |
| **Mean of the four** | | **0.92** | 0.77 (AdarEdit) |

ModFormer is close to the best method on each specialist's home ground, and far ahead on mouse.
Its lowest score across the four, 0.83, is higher than every other method's mean.

## One model transfers across species and substrates

<img src="/projects/modformer/fig2.webp" alt="Figure 2 of ModFormer: editing-site AUC and rate correlation on five test sets, zero-shot and after one epoch of fine-tuning, against training ablations and other architectures." className="lit-figure" />

The base model was pretrained on human data only: REDIportal sites and HEK293FT long-read
measurements. Without seeing a mouse example, it scores mouse Neuro-2a sites at an AUC of 0.96.
One epoch of joint fine-tuning over eight sources then helps most where the base model was
weakest. On invertebrate duplexes the AUC rises from 0.75 to 0.82, and the correlation between
predicted and measured rates rises from 0.48 to 0.66.

## It picks up how ADAR chooses its substrates

<img src="/projects/modformer/fig3.webp" alt="Figure 3 of ModFormer: recovery of highly edited Alu sites, sequence and opposing-base enrichment around high-scoring adenosines, and predicted effects of creating or removing mismatches opposite the adenosine." className="lit-figure" />

Ranked by ModFormer, highly edited Alu sites come up sooner than when ranked by the language model
alone. High-scoring adenosines carry the sequence and opposing-base preferences known for ADAR.
In computational edits, the model responds to creating or removing a mismatch opposite the
adenosine more than to nearby changes that keep the pairing.

## It predicts what a mutation does to editing

<img src="/projects/modformer/fig4.webp" alt="Figure 4: variant effects on RNA modification, with correlation across five cellular conditions and prioritization of responsive sites." className="lit-figure" />

A library of thousands of mutated RNA duplexes, expressed in cells and measured, provides
ground truth for what a mutation does to modification rates. The model predicts which sites
respond, and in which direction, well enough to pick responsive sites before the experiment.
Across five cellular conditions, its top-ranked sites show increased modification more often
than random, and the effect holds for endogenous substrates.

## Try it on any gene in a few seconds

The command line scores a gene by name, with optional species, isoform, and cellular context:

```bash
modformer APP --region 67248-69295       # score human APP pre-mRNA
modformer APP --species mouse --site 1494
```

```python
import modformer
table = modformer.predict(sequence)      # one row per adenosine
```

Each row gives a score (resemblance to observed sites, 0 to 1) and a predicted rate (0 to 1).
Install with one line on Linux or Apple-silicon Mac, Python 3.10 to 3.14:

```bash
curl -LsSf https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nygctech/ModFormer/main/install.sh | sh
```
