One of the greatest challenges agentic coders are facing, in the short term, is control and insight over workflows that scale far beyond the capabilities of a 
single individual in linear time. Many of us are working on interfaces that help alleviate this strain in our own work. This is one of my attempts: a custom 
ccstatusline that provides as much high level information as possible, and full navigational connectivity to facilitate switching between many 
tasks and sessions. The result is a more intuitive feel for those used to cd-ing around in the terminal, but, instead of directories, navigation is 
across agent contexts.

## Digestible information at all times

Below you can see the typical layout. It is designed with a retro/sci-fi feel across two rows at the bottom of the terminal. The top row
describes where an agent is performing its work. The bottom row gives usage and cost information. Each session is embodied by a unique creature that is intended
to relay nuanced information about the status of each session, similar to reading emotional context from a human collaborator's face.

## A collaborator you can see

Claude has rapidly become a near-constant work companion for many, yet in a terminal it has no physical 
presence beyond scrolling text. We become familiar with the characteristics of a session by its behavior,
but we could just as easily learn about it from a set of visual descriptors describing what it is doing,
if it is stuck, if it has nothing to do. An embodied pixel creature in every session provides a simple
version of this. It walks while Claude works, waves when the turn is mine, turns amber
and then red as its context fills, and falls asleep when left alone. You can toggle through the states here.

## Under the hood

One file of standard-library Python, about 3,000 lines, no dependencies.

- **Each creature is generated from its session id**, grown or built from parts, then packed into braille.
- **Mood comes from the session's activity**, never the words in it.
- **Every reading is a hyperlink.** A click goes through tmux to a small handler that checks it before acting:
