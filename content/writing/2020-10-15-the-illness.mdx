A sliver of light slices its way through my heavy eyelids and works its way through the internal mechanisms that make up my mind.  Through the pipes and across the cables, following a tortuous path, the light finds its way to its mysterious home.  An explosive force renders me breathless as my head splits into two.  Half I hold in one hand, the other flops to the side.   The shrieks in my head slowly form into moans.  The moans are growing louder, becoming hoarser in tone.  A shudder, a spasm, electrical patterns are now all awry.  Clenching and thrashing while traveling through space.  I am a tree, a bird, myself.  I am my mother, my brother, my sister, and son.  Thousands of lives are flickering past.  Faster and faster, the images scream. I am a drill bit and a coyote and a cigarette; now I’m a sun!

A final gasp, and my mind returns to me.  I am lying in bed, fully clothed, with my lower body grotesquely contorted and partially dangling in the air.  A brief glance towards the foot of the bed brings a dresser shaped blob into view.  I try to focus on the dust-armored fan perched precariously on the corner.  The normal hum is a cacophony now.  I feel the breeze brush over my sweat-drenched chest.  The machine continues tracing its arc.  Back and forth.  The gears, hidden in their plastic frame, skip three beats with every pass to the left.  I am not aware of this though; at the moment I am aware of nothing more than primitive perception of the sensory stimuli associated with my room.  No objects have been labeled with meaning and colors are not post-processed into the usual continuum of shades.  I simply see the world as it exists, my human understanding no longer imposed on it.

I blink, and the world slips into darkness for a brief moment before returning to me once again.  Slowly the visual stimuli arrange themselves.  The auditory ones remain jumbled.  I feel I am gradually sinking back into my body; exhausted from the long journey to a destination of which I am not privy to know.

*Something is wrong.  I am not right.*  These are the first thoughts I remember understanding since the night before.  Yes, I am quite ill.  I can feel it now.  A quick systems check: the eyes, those are working; let’s try the hands – nothing – no matter, we’ll come back to those; wriggle the toes, yes I believe that is good; wait now there’s a new pain, a stabbing like needles, all up my shin; back to the fingers, yes some small movement now; tilt the head to the left and … suddenly the world spins around.  Briefly I feel that I’m hanging upside down.  Then slowly I roll back around.  *Let’s not do that again for a minute or two.*

I lie in absolute stillness, a corpse with a mind.  At least my awareness of who I am has returned; I seem to be mostly here.  Maybe an internal review should have been where I started.  Who am I? *I am Lawrence F. Telvon.*  Good, that seems right.  How old am I?  *33*.  Yep.  Where do I live?  *137 Capital Street*.  Right again.  Now for something harder.  Where do I work?  *I’m a test organism at SynthaCorp…wait no; why did I say that?*  Confusion again.  The edges of comprehension begin to blur.  All things associated with all other things.  Darkness encroaches on the edges of my vision, the light slowly fading inwards towards the center.  I focus hard and force the darkness back to the periphery.  Where do I work?  *I’m a janitor at the manufacturing complex downtown.*  Yes, I feel much more certain about this.

My thoughts are beginning to spontaneously self-assemble and increase in complexity.  I decide to try the bodily assessment again.  Left hand first.  The fingers respond this time.  I curl them into a loose approximation of a fist.  Right hand.  Same response.  Moving to the legs.  Toes, still good.  Both feet back and forth.  Mimicking the motion of my fan.  I lift each leg at the knee, and I’m surprised at the lack of protestation from my body.  I believe I might be able to get up now.  *All systems go.*

I lower my feet to the floor and slowly rise up to sitting.  The movement releases a previously corroded gear somewhere in the back of my head that is now spinning out of control.  I steady myself and cease to do any voluntary information processing for the next thirty seconds.  The machinery driving my consciousness begins to regain control of the system.  Suddenly, in a flash, as though the realization had been building up behind a dam in my mind that without warning has crumbled, I remember – *I am not well*.  Something is incredibly wrong.  I felt normal yesterday, all systems functioning nominally.  What happened?

I run through the timeline of the day before.  Woke up.  Nothing abnormal.  Plugged into the console for a quick bit of fun.  Beaches in Chile.  Sunshine and women and laughing with rum.  *God, I love VR*.  I snap back; normal, all normal.  Went to work.  I never remember much of what happens there.  They are starting a new assembly line.  I think I cleaned up an organic contamination from facility F.  Or was that last week?  *Why do my levers and gaskets keep failing*?

I remain sitting, completely stationary.  My eyelids sagging but unblinking.  The fan again passes through the angle that best aligns the moving molecules of the airstream with my overheated body.  I can feel the pump in my chest increasing its stroke rate in response to my feelings of unease.

A memory has reappeared somewhere in my head.  What is it?  It seems to be important.  Let me try to tease it out from whichever dark corner it is hiding in.  Profuse sweating, body with extra electricity.  Couldn’t move.  All of these activate some recognition in me, but of what?  Does it relate to me?  *I don’t think so.*  To Cindy?  *No, definitely not.  And why do you keep thinking of her?  There’s no chance.  Don’t torture… Focus!*  A family member maybe?  *I don’t know anything about my sisters.  I don’t think they are doing well though.*  That’s not it.  *Dad?*  An extreme heaviness falls over me, and I know I’ve found the connection.  No wonder it was hiding.  I’ve tried very hard not to think of him for nearly 30 years.  But it’s all still there.  The shaking; the blood; even the look in his eyes as his processing gears ceased to function, one by one.  *Yes, I believe this is how it started.*  Involuntarily, I relive two years of despair, compressed into two minutes.  His sadness.  The pain.  Him making sure to explain everything as it happened.  The gears with broken teeth.  The drive train malfunctioning.  Of course, he didn’t say it, but I know the primary dysfunction: fouled bodily lubricating fluids, had to be.  Transistors and pulleys and resistors and cogs and capacitors don’t malfunction that way otherwise.  *Dad taught me that.*  *Even then he was looking out for me.*  I understand my own body.  Lot of people don’t.  Still think they are organic, made up of make-believe words like protein and collagen and deoxyribonucleic acid and such.  *They do not know the truth.*  So many people do not understand themselves.

I tilt my right toes toward the ceiling to make sure they still work.  *Yep.*  A shudder of excitement proves everything else is still working too.  Another memory has surfaced.

When my father stopped functioning, I remember them coming.  The white coats and masks and gloves.  They collected his body, for research they said.  Some nonsense about genetic malfunction they said.  With studies of his machinery they must have made progress.  That was so long ago.  I feel the heaviness lessen to a slight degree.  The despair that had been creeping in now kept at bay.  *I must contact one of those doctor type folks!*

The joy is tempered even before the moment has passed.  *I cannot remember if I signed a waiver this year.*  I never understand the documents they put in front of me.  I’ve never had a need to go to one of these places.  The boss just says they take my money in case I get sick one day.  *I am sick today.*  Let me just call them up to see where to go; there’s only one company that does this type of thing.  My mind wanders again as I reconstruct the small portion of the universe that passes in front of my stereoscopic photon receptors on each walk to work.  The sights and sounds scamper through my thoughts.  I finally arrive at my mental destination.  A sign, a large billboard next to the sky bridge.  I know it well.  Yellow with blue letters, some numbers too.  The vision fades into my awareness.  “Helping you is our passion,” these letters say.  *I could certainly use some help now*.

My phone is on the other side of the bare room.  A single outlet, with a single cord, and my phone sitting on the floor.  I make a move toward it, but instead find myself transferred straight into hell.  My mind disappears briefly from existence.  I collapse onto the concrete next to my bed.  Now I am retching, heaving up the contents of my consumption reservoir.  Two cheeseburgers and a beer from yesterday, partially dissolved but still recognizable.  The hot acid moves from pipe to pipe.  Stinging my throat, coming out through my nose.  I’m choking.  Precious air no longer enters my body.  I contort and I heave.  Writhing and gurgling in the spot where I fell.

I remain lying on my side long after the heaving has subsided.  A pile of vomit serving as a pillow.  Eventually, I remember my goal.  The phone, ten feet away.  Help is ten feet away.  I just need to make it.

I begin crawling, slowly dragging my body through the contents that were previously destined to be broken down and turned into the fuel to keep me functioning.  Eight feet left and another heave wracks my body.  I keep moving.  Five feet, and a violent spasm.  My head slams against the floor.  I see red and then black.  All the pain I’ve ever experienced is now focused in my head, all at once.  I fall still and don’t know how long I remain there.

Piece by piece.  Item by item.  I am slowly reassembled once more.  I stare blankly at the fan as each memory is carefully slotted back into its usual home.  Back and forth.  I watch the fan a little longer, mesmerized by its pattern.  Only gradually do I recall why I am lying in a pool of blood on my bedroom floor.  I inch forward, straining from the effort.  I have lost track of my arm.  *There it is*.  Slowly I slide it up, leaving an arc of red that ends at my phone.

It greets me with its customary joy at first, then squawks with alarm when it doesn’t recognize me.  *My face is too deformed*.  I feebly try to convince it I am indeed Lawrence, its friend.  Skepticism remains but it agrees to let me make a call.  A half-formed thought crosses my mind.  There used to be an emergency number; not anymore.  Too expensive or something.  *I think I voted to get rid of that too.*

Who to call?  Ah yes, I remember now.  The friendly people on the sign.  I’ve passed it every day, or permutations of it, for nearly a decade.  Yellow now, last year green; red for a long time before that.  Even in my wretched state I remember the number and ask my phone to call.  Ringing.  Relief courses through my body.  I practice speaking to make sure I am still able.  *‘Hello.’*  The effort brings on a new wave of nausea.

“Hello, helping you is our passion.”

“*HELLO!”* I shout just like rehearsed.

“Please listen carefully as our menu options have changed.”

I sigh and listen through the options.  Locations: maybe.  Claims: also not sure.  File a compliment: probably not.  List of healthy habits: not right now.  Technical help: sounds kind of promising.  I hear the words for the last four options but can’t comprehend their meaning.   I listen through the list two more times.  I eventually choose a number at random.  Seven.

More ringing and then a computer answers with a carefully programmed, caring excitement.  Another list of options.  I use all the concentration left in my body to listen for the option that seems most likely to take me to a person.  Three menu cycles later I hear the word ‘emergency’ and punch in the corresponding numeric combination.

I find myself drifting in and out once again.  The music floats up from the speaker beside me; it mixes with my thoughts and takes the shape of various wild, animal shaped shadows.  They dance around me.

“Hello?”  A voice cuts through my delirium and drags me back to the sickly-scented scene around me.  “Helping you is our passion.  How can we assist you today?”

I weakly moan and then gather myself for the tremendous effort ahead. *“I am very sick.  My lubricating fluids are all junked up.  Same thing happened to my dad.  I think they used him to help find a fix.  I’m real bad right now.  Please help me.”*  The last sentence gurgles out unrecognizably.

“Thank you for calling us sir.  Can I get your member ID?”

*“I’m not sure.  I work at… I don’t remember.”*

“Unfortunately, I cannot help you if you are not a member of our community.  And it would be unfair to those who are members.”

*“Let me give you my name, and I know my employee ID at…wherever I work.  I still know my ID.”*  I give both to the monotone voice on the other end.

I hear typing and then a long, complete silence.  It is only interrupted by a spasmodic coughing fit on my end.  I’ve choked on some blood and vomit still in my mouth.

“Okay, yes Mr. Telvon.  You are a member.  You are, however, a part of our lowest tier.  Per the stipulations of your policy, which do not cover emergency phone calls, I will begin charging you for this conversation, and the charges will be retroactive to when you first dialed us.  We also have on file that you did not complete your annual waiver of our responsibility for medical mishap which means most of your services have been suspended until you can come into our office to sign the form.  We perform those services every Thursday from 1:00pm- 2:00pm Greenwich Mean Time.  How can I help you today?”

Most of what the phone has just said doesn’t mean anything to me, so I ignore it.  *“I got a serious problem.  My lubricating fluids are fouled, and I’m electrically hyperactive.  I’ve been expelling my food and think I’m bleeding now too.  My whole body is in pain.”*  I take a deep breath and wince, but continue slowly.

*“Like I was saying before, same thing happened to my dad.  Just like this, but a little slower.  Some people said they were gonna use him to figure this thing out.  Figure they probably have by now, so was hoping they could use what they learned by taking my dad’s machinery apart and help fix me.  I’m too early for this kind of gear failure.”*

“I’m sorry sir, you are not making any sense.  What is this about machinery and gears and fluids?  Is one of your home bots malfunctioning?”

*“No, I ain’t got a home bot.  This is about me.  My machinery is fouled, just like my dad’s!”*

“I’m sorry sir, what are you referring to by lubricating fluids and gears?  You are not making any sense.  Are you trying to receive help for a malfunctioning synthetic appliance?  If so, I am afraid you are talking with the wrong corporation.”

I groan audibly.  Another person, no sense of self.  *Probably a believer about what they try to say about internal parts.*  Not the time.  I need help.  *“No, not one of the synthetic machines.  ME.  I am shutting do… I mean dying.  I am dying!”*

The cold voice, briefly rattled by the perceived misunderstanding, immediately begins, “Your account does not come with complimentary medical advice.  If you would like me to transfer you to a concierge medical professional for pay-as-you-go medical guidance, I will be happy to transfer you now.”

*“Please,”* I wheeze.

“Transferring you now.  Your total for this conversation comes to…” My auditory receptors fade momentarily.  “Thanks to our convenient relationship with your employer, this will be automatically withheld from your paycheck until paid in full.  No need for further action by you!  Helping you is our passion.  Have a good day.”

I am too tired to cry.  The left side of my head is now completely numb.  The cold concrete no longer stings my cheek.  I’m briefly confused about whether or not I still have a cheek.  I lie there in exhaustion.  Unable to move.  I lose track of the time as I wait for another voice to come out of the phone.  I slowly move my gaze from my phone to the small window over my bed.  Maybe my vision is fading again.  It seems night is already approaching.  My mind returns to blankness.

“Hi, hello?”  I open my eyes and scream I’m here.  “Oh, well, yes, hello!”  A jovial voice booms from the phone.  “My name is Dr. Sanford.  I understand you are seeking some pay-as-you-go medical advice.  Our automated triage service paired your complaints with my expertise!  Are you familiar with the pay structure?”

I grunt.

“Well, it’s actually quite simple sir!  You pay a baseline fee per minute for our conversation, really a quite reasonable cost per minute.  It is remarkable how low of a price we can provide high quality medical advice to some of our lowest tier members.  It took us nearly ten years to figure out how to get it so low!  Had to get some massive subsidies from the government to make it financially reasonable!”  He chuckles heartily, obviously pleased with himself, for what feels like ten minutes.  I am having a difficult time remembering what people are referring to when they use terms like dollars and fee and cash and payment.  I feel sure it has something to do with my job, but the world is too foggy for me to be overly concerned.

The phone voice finishes his chuckling episode and continues, “On top of the baseline fee, we also give you the price of each piece of medical advice before we reveal it to you, so that you can decide what is worth paying for!  It’s brilliant!  So how can I advise you today?”

I launch into the description I gave the previous voice and end with a plea I feel will hit home. *“I’m really scared.  I saw this happen to my dad and I know when you guys took him you said it would help you stop it from happening to me one day.  I just remembered that just now.  One of the people, in a blue gown, he said they could use his machinery to find a way to fix mine, if mine ever went haywire.  Well it is now.  I’m sure y’all have got something figured out now!”*

A pause, some deep breathing, and then a slow response.  “What are you referring to when you speak of fluids and levers and gears?  Are you talking about your own body?”

“*Yes, of course.  I know my body.  I’m telling you it’s fouled.”*

The man’s voice changes with his next words.  The joyful tone muted, as though he did not intend to relay his words all the way to where I lie.  “God help us all.  Not again.”

I say nothing.

The jovial version of his voice booms back louder than before.  “Hey, this is crazy but I’ve got a question for you!  Your dad lived on Capital Street?”

*“YES”* I nearly scream.  He knows, he knows!  I’m saved!

“Well, damn.  I was part of that team.  Worked on his case too!  We learned so much from him.  Actually, basically built my career off the research we published from his case.  Crazy small world, huh!  Good news is I know exactly what is going on here!”

I am now nearly on the verge of tears.  I have found help.  These guys kept their promise after all.  *“Thannk, thank youuu, thank you so much,”* I stammer.

<em>“</em>Okay, what would you like to pay for today?  A medical diagnosis costs…” a jumble of noises, “…and if you opt for the additional informational description of the diagnosis, we will bundle it all together for eighty-five.  At home treatment suggestions are double that (though I’ll give you some free advice here, don’t choose that one).  If you would like to travel to one of our facilities, we guarantee we can treat you for anywhere between…” some typing, “… 10 and 15 years of your salary.  Of course, if we need to pick you up, you can essentially double that price.  What will it be today?”  His noisy breaths can be heard again as he waits for a reply.

A sticky cogwheel somewhere deep in my head remains stuck.  I’m not sure what the phone has just said.  A convulsion shakes my body and all my restored memories flee, with the exception of the ones including my father.  *“I..I…can’t,”* I manage to say.  *“What about my father, you took him.  He was just carried away.  You said you could fix me if I needed.”*

“Yes of course we can fix you, do not doubt that!  I will need you to sign some documentation however to verify that you are willing and able to pay.  It would be incredibly unfair to our upper tier members if you were to receive free medical advice.  Are you willing to sign the documents?”

*“I…I…don’t think I have any money.  I’m not sure.”*

“Oh, do not worry we also have a couple additional options to help you take out a special, emergency, high interest loan if you can’t get the cash immediately.  I can have you speak to a financial counselor if you would like to pursue that route?  That’s not my area of expertise, but I do believe we can get you approved in as little as 7-14 business days!”

An overwhelming feeling of despair descends over my entire body.  I can actually feel it, the despair.  In my head, in my throat, in my stomach and all of my limbs.  I summon the remainder of my strength to look back at the window.  It is pitch black outside.

“I guess I don’t have any other option, let’s…”  I am interrupted by a high-pitched beeping coming from my phone.  The heavy breathing on the other end ceases.  A message flashes across the screen.  Only barely visible from my vantage point on the floor.  I close my left eye so my right eye can focus from its higher vantage point.

I slowly read the words.

> *Mr. Lawrence F. Telvon we regret that you did not*\
> *report to work today.  We also regret that you did*\
> *not inform us of your unexcused absence.  As a re-*\
> *sult, we have contacted your media provider in or-*\
> *der to suspend your phone and media access unti-*\
> *l you correct your infractions.  Continued laziness*\
> *will result in your final termination.*\
> *\[%3\[*\
> *SynthaCorp Management*

I heave a great sigh and a shudder starts at my toes.  It runs all the way up my body, climaxing at my head with a violent thrashing motion.  Back and forth.  A roar fills my ears, the room fades away.  A crash, a blur, and the me of myself, simply melts away.  I leave the earth; I feel my body struck by lightning.  Images fly by and I feel like I’m fighting.  I am wrestling a man.  The man was me but also my mother.  Fighting and rolling and struggling now.  I’m a dog and tree and a dolphin, now a cow.  Everyone is flying the earth is so pretty.  I see all the people that I’ve wanted to be.  The colors are one, I feel a warmth all over my body.  From deep down inside, one final click.  The images fade; the noise dies away.  The world is silence, nothing exists.  I float downward, a descent into bliss.  Darkness and blankness, and then, nothing.
